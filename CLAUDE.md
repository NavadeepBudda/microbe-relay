# Ocean Denitrification: Microbial Relay Science

## Project Purpose
Interactive website teaching how ocean microbes share denitrification work. Users control food/oxygen sliders to see which microbe specialists dominate and when greenhouse gases spike. Designed for high school students and general public.

## Core Science: Modular Specialists
In low-oxygen ocean zones, microbes "breathe" nitrogen through **denitrification**: nitrate → nitrite → nitrous oxide → nitrogen gas. Most microbes are specialists doing only 1-2 steps, not the full pathway.

## Competition Rules
- **Low food**: Short pathways win (Step 1 dominates)
- **High food**: Multi-step specialists win (complete pathways)
- **Medium food**: Multiple specialists coexist (N₂O hotspots)

## Key Learning Outcomes
1. **Food scarcity** = first step (nitrate→nitrite) dominates
2. **Food abundance** = multi-step microbes take over  
3. **Food pulses** = N₂O greenhouse gas spikes
4. **Specialists coexist** because later steps depend on earlier ones

## Environmental Controls
- **Low food**: Step 1 dominant, NO₂⁻ main product, low N₂O
- **Medium food**: Multiple coexist, N₂O peaks, greenhouse gas hotspots  
- **High food**: Complete pathways, full N₂ conversion, max N loss

## Climate Impact
- **N₂O production** peaks when multiple modules coexist
- **Ocean nitrogen loss** depends on which specialists dominate
- **Food supply** is the master variable controlling both

## Website Teaching Goals
Users learn that microbe competition for food/nitrogen determines:
- Which denitrification steps happen
- When/where greenhouse gases are produced  
- How ocean fertility and climate are connected
- Why ocean variability matters for global cycles
